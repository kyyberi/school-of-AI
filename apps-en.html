Starter app gallery

What kids can build

Teams do not start from a blank screen. They choose a working starter app, understand it, change it, improve it and make it their own.

The Sunday goal is simple: show something real that the team built together.

Beginner

First changes should be visible quickly.

English Word Buddy

A bilingual vocabulary app with English words, Vietnamese meanings and example sentences that teams can turn into their own quiz or glossary.

My World Map

An interactive map with markers, images and descriptions that teams can adapt into a places, food, travel or history map.

Story Builder

A random story starter where users choose characters, places and themes, then rewrite the templates into their own creative writing tool.

Mini Website Builder

A polished one-page website template for a hobby, club, event, family business or personal project.

Mood and Activity Picker

A simple recommendation app that suggests activities based on how the user feels and what they want to do.

Medium

More logic, APIs and game mechanics.

English Speaking Challenge

A browser speaking game where learners say English phrases and receive simple recognition feedback and scoring.

Treasure Hunt Map

A map game where players read clues, click the correct place and earn points based on accuracy.

Smart Study Planner

A homework and study tracker with tasks, due dates, priorities and saved progress in the browser.

Quiz Battle

A multiple-choice quiz app with score tracking, progress and a final result screen that teams can retheme.

Camera Vocabulary Helper

A camera or image-based vocabulary helper that connects objects to English words, Vietnamese meanings and example sentences.

A community learning idea starting in Yên Bái and aiming for more locations.

Educational disclaimer

School4AI is an independent community learning initiative.

It is not part of the official school curriculum in Vietnam and is not affiliated with the Ministry of Education and Training or any local education authority unless explicitly stated.

The activities are informal and practical. They are intended to complement, not replace, formal education.
